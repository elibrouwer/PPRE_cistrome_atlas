# PPRE score v4: design for a publishable method

Status: **draft for discussion, written 2026-10-08, before any v4 model is fitted.** Nothing here has been run. v3 stays as the main comparator and is not changed.
Scope: human data only. Target: a method-plus-resource paper (for example NAR / NAR Genomics and Bioinformatics / Bioinformatics). The released catalogue of all 42-motif hits stays the core resource; v4 is the prioritisation layer on top.

## 1. The claim v4 is allowed to make (and the ones it is not)
**Claim to test:** *Combining motif evidence with cardiac chromatin accessibility and conservation, and assigning sites to genes beyond the promoter window, ranks PPAR/RXR-responsive genes better than motif-only scores, accessibility alone, and a published-style affinity score, on human data that was never used to build or tune the model.*
**Not claimed:** that high-scoring genes are PPAR targets in the heart, that the score predicts disease effects, or anything about PPARA specifically (no usable human PPARA binding data exists).

## 2. What v3 taught us (the design constraints)
1. The old sum mostly measures promoter size; beating it is not enough for a paper. v4 must beat **simple, strong baselines**.
2. GC content alone predicts ChIP peaks about as well as motif strength (HUVEC: hit GC 0.77, flank GC 0.72, v3 0.70). Any binding claim must be made **within GC bands** and against a GC-aware baseline.
3. v3 holds GC fixed and has no cell-type information; PPAR/RXR occupancy needs motif **and** open chromatin in the relevant cell type.
4. v3's promoter-only window misses distal binding. v3 was inverse at gene level in HUVEC (odds ratio 0.80) and for PPARA in HepG2.
5. Ligand-induced changes are mostly not in binding (HUVEC: 9 gained regions of 1,284) but in chromatin activity and transcription, so the **functional endpoint must be the transcriptional response**, not binding.
6. The cardiomyocyte snRNA-seq cluster effect was driven by signalling genes, not PPAR biology: snRNA-seq is a descriptive check, not a validation endpoint.

## 3. Model (kept deliberately small so it can be explained and audited)
**Stage 1, site model** (probability a hit is a functional PPRE-like site): logistic regression on binding data with
* motif strength: -log10 FIMO p, as in v3, clipped 4-9 (spline df 3);
* architecture class of the best motif (DR1-type, half-site, other), from the motif inventory, as a factor;
* local GC (flank, hit): **nuisance covariates held fixed at prediction time** (as v3), so the score carries only motif evidence;
* cardiac accessibility `A`: overlap with ENCODE heart left-ventricle ATAC (ENCFF174HPZ) and cardiac H3K27ac regions (both already in the project), as two binary covariates plus ATAC signal;
* conservation `C`: overlap with phastCons 100-way elements (already in the project).
Trained on PPARG/PPARD/RXRA ChIP-seq sets with a dataset indicator, odd chromosomes only. Ablation ladder reported for every component: strength only, + architecture, + accessibility, + conservation.
**Stage 2, gene linking:** each site contributes to every gene whose TSS lies within a window, with weight w(d) from a fixed, pre-specified grid: promoter (-3 kb to +1 kb) as in v3; proximal (up to 10 kb); extended (up to 50 kb). The window is a model setting chosen **on development data only** and then frozen. No enhancer-gene links beyond distance in the first version (none are available locally); a promoter-capture Hi-C layer is a possible extension, reported separately.
**Stage 3, aggregation:** de-duplicated loci (centres within 10 bp), noisy-OR across loci as in v3, then the length+GC (no TSS-term) residual so gene size is not scored.

## 4. Data and splits (fixed before looking at any result)
* **Binding (site level)**: PPARG adipocyte, digestive tract (HT-29, with rosiglitazone), blood (THP-1); PPARβ/δ HUVEC (GSE50144, with GW501516; mislabelled PPARG in ChIP-Atlas); ENCODE HepG2 PPARG (ENCSR130VQL); RXRA sets. Experiments with fewer than about 1,000 peaks are reported but not used for fitting.
* **Function (gene level)**: ligand-response gene sets from human agonist experiments. Available now: hPSC-cardiomyocytes, GW0742 / WY14643 / rosiglitazone (GSE160987, DESeq2 labels already built). To be added after approval of downloads: HUVEC GW501516 microarray (GSE50378); further human agonist RNA-seq from GEO (adipocyte or hepatocyte rosiglitazone / fenofibrate), each checked for replicates and a vehicle control. **At least three independent cell systems are needed for a leave-one-dataset-out design**; if fewer are found, the paper reports the functional validation as limited.
* **Independent literature check**: the 75 published PPRE sites / 58 genes (never used for fitting).
* **Splits**: (a) chromosome hold-out within a dataset (odd train / even test), (b) leave-one-dataset-out across binding sets, (c) leave-one-cell-system-out across functional sets. A locked test set (cardiomyocyte agonist response, GSE160987) is evaluated **once**, at the end.

## 5. Baselines and negative controls (what a reviewer will ask for)
Baselines, all computed on the same genes and splits:
1. best single FIMO hit; 2. hit count; 3. the original manuscript sum; 4. v3 (human-trained); 5. GC-only and promoter-length-only; 6. accessibility-only (ATAC signal at the promoter); 7. distance-to-TSS only; 8. cardiomyocyte expression only; 9. a **published-style affinity score**: sum of motif affinity weighted by accessibility within +-50 kb of the TSS (TEPIC-style, implemented from its published description; the project already holds a TEPIC reproduction script).
Negative controls: (i) shuffled-sequence and column-permuted motifs scored through the identical pipeline (expected: no gain); (ii) permuted outcome labels (1,000); (iii) random gene sets matched on length, GC and expression.
Robustness: motif set (all 42 vs the 25 reliable motifs, side analysis), hit gate (1e-4, 1e-5, 1e-6), window, with and without the TSS-count term.

## 6. Endpoints and success criteria (pre-registered)
**Primary (gene level, function):** AUROC for ligand-responsive genes (up in the agonist condition), with the adjusted odds ratio per SD (promoter length, GC, log TSS count, expression) as the companion, computed per cell system; 95% bootstrap CI over genes.
**v4 counts as a method result only if all of these hold:**
1. v4 beats the **best baseline among 1-9** by a difference whose 95% CI excludes 0 in at least 2 of the 3 held-out functional systems and does not lose in the third;
2. each added component (accessibility, conservation, extended window) improves the held-out score in the ablation ladder, or is dropped from the final model;
3. all negative controls give no gain (shuffled motifs, permuted labels);
4. binding endpoint: within-GC-band AUC beats FIMO p alone and GC-only on the held-out binding sets;
5. literature genes: AUROC at least that of v3 and of the best baseline (reported with CI).
**If these are not met**, the paper reports the motif catalogue plus the finding that standard motif-count scores mostly measure promoter size and that accessibility explains most of the achievable signal. That is still a publishable resource and negative-result statement; v4 is then reported as an ablation, not as a method.

## 7. Reporting standards
* Every threshold, window and seed fixed here; deviations listed in a deviations table, never silent.
* All development decisions made on development sets only; the locked set touched once; its result reported whatever it is.
* Release: all FIMO hits (resource), the per-hit probabilities, gene scores for all 19,332 genes, code and environment file, run on a tagged release with a DOI.
* Limitations section written up front: no human cardiomyocyte PPAR/RXR ChIP exists; PPARA is not represented in the binding data; ligand-response labels include indirect effects; tissue ATAC mixes cell types; the model is trained on PPARG/PPARD binding.

## Addendum 1 (2026-10-08, written before the run): expression-aware v4 ("v4e")
Motivation: step 3 of RESULT_GATE1.md showed that apparent gains from chromatin gating were an expression effect. In the intended use (ranking genes expressed in cardiomyocytes) the correction for promoter length/GC/#TSS should be learned on expressed genes, not on all 19,332 genes.
**Definition.** v4e = the v3 raw noisy-OR score R (42 motifs, gate 1e-5, promoter window, human-trained site model) residualised on length + GC + log #TSS (same spline terms as v3) with the residual model **fitted only on expressed genes** and the score evaluated **only on expressed genes**. Expressed = mean log2 array expression above the median of the dataset's mapped genes. Comparator: v3 (residual fitted on all genes) evaluated on the same expressed genes.
**Test.** Two development contrasts (GSE17251 Wy14643 24 h; GSE53399 GW7647 24 h 10 uM), labels = top 5% and 1% of paired t among expressed genes (4 contrasts). Metrics: AUROC with paired bootstrap difference (v4e minus v3, 500 resamples, genes resampled), adjusted OR per SD (length, GC, #TSS, expression).
**Success rule (fixed now):** v4e counts as an improvement only if the paired AUROC difference has a 95% CI above 0 in at least 3 of the 4 contrasts. Otherwise v4e is reported as equal to v3 and dropped. No other variants, thresholds or definitions of "expressed" will be tried; the result is reported either way.

### Addendum 1 result (2026-10-08): v4e fails the pre-set rule
Code `v4e_expressed.py`, table `v4e_results.tsv`. Among expressed genes (8,302), paired AUROC difference v4e minus v3: +0.001 (-0.001 to 0.004), +0.002 (-0.003 to 0.006), 0.000 (-0.003 to 0.003), -0.002 (-0.008 to 0.004); 0 of 4 contrasts improved (rule: at least 3). Adjusted OR identical (1.09 vs 1.09, 1.30 vs 1.31, 1.23 vs 1.24, 1.42 vs 1.42). v4e is equal to v3 and dropped. Useful side fact: restricted to expressed genes, v3 itself has AUROC 0.53-0.58 and adjusted OR 1.09-1.42.

## Addendum 2 (2026-10-08, written before the run): training-free score ("TF")
Question: does learning the hit weights from ChIP-seq (adipocyte PPARG) matter? If a score with fixed, assumed weights does as well, the "trained on non-cardiac cells" concern disappears.
**Definition.** Same hits (42 motifs, gate 1e-5), promoter window (-3000..+1000), loci merged within 10 bp, noisy-OR per gene, length+GC+log #TSS residual as in v3. Only the per-hit probability changes: q = 0.15 x f(s) x w(d), with f(s) = (s - 4)/5 clipped to 0-1 (s = -log10 FIMO p clipped 4-9) and a fixed distance weight. **TF-A (primary):** w = exp(-|d|/3000), the decay assumed in the original manuscript score. **TF-B (secondary):** w = 1 (no distance information). The constants 0.15, 4-9 and 3000 are fixed here and not tuned.
**Tests (same as v3).** (1) hepatocyte response, GSE17251 and GSE53399, expressed genes only, top 5% and 1% (4 contrasts): paired AUROC difference TF minus v3 with 500-resample bootstrap; (2) 75 published PPRE sites (tiers 1-3): AUROC; (3) gene-level binding in 6 human ChIP sets (promoter peak overlap, adjusted OR per SD for length, GC, #TSS; PPARG adipocyte on even chromosomes only).
**Rule (fixed now).** TF-A counts as "equal to v3" (learning from ChIP adds little) if (a) the lower 95% bound of the paired AUROC difference is above -0.01 in at least 3 of 4 hepatocyte contrasts, (b) literature AUROC is within 0.03 of v3, and (c) its adjusted OR is at least 0.9 times that of v3 in at least 4 of 6 ChIP sets. If not, the learned hit weights are kept and reported as useful. TF-B is reported for information and does not enter the rule.

### Addendum 2 result (2026-10-08): the training-free score equals v3
Code `v4tf.py`, table `v4tf_results.tsv`. Ranking: Spearman 0.96 with v3 (TF-A), 0.90 (TF-B). Hepatocyte response, paired AUROC difference TF-A minus v3: +0.009 (0.001 to 0.016), +0.006 (-0.008 to 0.020), +0.001 (-0.005 to 0.008), +0.001 (-0.017 to 0.020); lower bound above -0.01 in 3 of 4 (rule: 3). Literature AUROC 0.585 vs 0.595 (diff -0.011, rule: within 0.03). ChIP gene-level adjusted OR ratio TF-A/v3 0.99-1.02 in 6 of 6 sets (rule: 4 of 6). **Rule met: TF-A is equal to v3.** Reading: the ChIP-learned hit weights barely change the ranking; a fixed, assumed-weight score does the same job. Both are weak predictors (see RESULT_GATE1.md). TF-B (no distance information) is about as good.

## Addendum 3 (2026-10-08, written before the run): two tissue-independent evidence layers on top of TF-A
Baseline: TF-A (Addendum 2). Same hits, window, noisy-OR, residual. Only the per-hit probability gets a fixed multiplier m, otherwise q = 0.15 x f(s) x exp(-|d|/3000) x m.
* **CONS:** m = 1 if the hit overlaps a phastCons 100-way element (UCSC table in the project), else 0.5.
* **DIMER:** a locus (hits merged within 10 bp) is "dimer-supported" if it holds hits from a PPAR-type model and an RXR-type model, with heterodimer models (MA0065, MA1148) counting as both. m = 1 if dimer-supported, else 0.5. (Most of the 42 PWMs are 14-20 bp full-site models, so a half-site spacing rule is not meaningful; model-family agreement at one locus is the testable form of the PPAR:RXR heterodimer idea.)
* CONS+DIMER (product of both multipliers) is reported for information only.
Constants (0.5, 1.0) are fixed here, not tuned. Tests: same three as Addendum 2 (4 hepatocyte contrasts, expressed genes; literature genes; 6 ChIP sets gene-level adjusted OR).
**Rule per layer (CONS and DIMER separately):** counts as an improvement over TF-A only if (a) the paired AUROC difference (layer minus TF-A) has its 95% lower bound above 0 in at least 3 of 4 hepatocyte contrasts, (b) literature AUROC is not more than 0.01 below TF-A, and (c) adjusted OR ratio layer/TF-A is at least 1.0 in at least 4 of 6 ChIP sets. Otherwise the layer is dropped. Result reported either way.

### Addendum 3 result (2026-10-08): neither layer meets the rule
Code `v4cd.py`, table `v4cd_results.tsv`. 18.2% of loci are dimer-supported (PPAR-type plus RXR-type hit); 17.6% of hits are in a phastCons element. Ranking stays close to TF-A (Spearman 0.94 CONS, 0.95 DIMER).
* Hepatocyte response (rule a: lower bound above 0 in 3 of 4): CONS 0 of 4 (differences -0.007 to +0.003); DIMER 1 of 4 (+0.002 to +0.020; only GSE53399 top 1% clear, 0.605 vs 0.585); CONS+DIMER 0 of 4.
* Literature genes: CONS +0.007, DIMER +0.002, CONS+DIMER +0.016 (rule b met).
* ChIP gene-level adjusted OR ratio vs TF-A: CONS 0.995-1.045 (5 of 6 at least 1), DIMER 1.007-1.077 (6 of 6), CONS+DIMER 1.017-1.113 (6 of 6; rule c met).
**Verdict: CONS and DIMER are dropped from the score under the pre-set rule (functional criterion not met).** Reported pattern: both layers raise binding prediction a little in every ChIP set (DIMER most clearly, up to +8% in odds ratio) but do not improve ligand-response prediction; this fits an improvement in "where PPAR/RXR can bind" that does not carry over to "which genes respond". Not adopted, since adopting it after seeing this pattern would be choosing on the binding result.

## Addendum 4 (2026-10-08, written before the run): robustness, per-gene intervals, leftover confounds for TF-A
Score under test: TF-A (training-free, Addendum 2): q = 0.15 x f(s) x exp(-|d|/3000), 42 motifs, hit gate 1e-5, window -3000..+1000, loci merged within 10 bp, noisy-OR, length+GC+log#TSS residual.
**A. Robustness grid (one change at a time from the default).** Hit gate 1e-4 and 1e-6; aggregation best-hit (max q per gene) and sum of q over loci; window -1000..+500 and -10000..+3000 (same decay); motif set = the 25 reliable motifs of the side analysis. 8 variants + default. Metrics per variant: Spearman with default; AUROC in the 4 hepatocyte contrasts (expressed genes, top 5% and 1%); literature AUROC; adjusted OR in 6 ChIP sets.
**Stability rule (fixed now):** a variant is "stable" if Spearman with default is at least 0.8 and its AUROC is within 0.02 of the default in all 4 hepatocyte contrasts. Variants are not ranked or selected; every one is reported, and the default stays the default whatever the outcome.
**B. Per-gene interval.** Poisson bootstrap over loci (200 draws, weights ~ Poisson(1) per locus) with the default residual model held fixed; interval = 2.5-97.5 percentile of the gene's percentile rank. Reported: median interval width; share of genes whose interval is wider than 30 percentile points; share of top-2000 genes that stay in the top 2000 in at least 80% of draws. The released table gets percentile, lower and upper bounds.
**C. Leftover confounds.** Spearman of the final score with promoter CpG observed/expected, CpG island overlap fraction, and gene density (other TSS within 50 kb). A covariate with |rho| > 0.1 is flagged and added (linear term) to the residual model as TF-A+. TF-A+ replaces TF-A only if no hepatocyte AUROC falls by more than 0.01 and literature AUROC falls by no more than 0.01; otherwise the leftover correlation is reported as a limitation.

### Addendum 4 result (2026-10-08)
Code `v4robust.py`, log `v4robust.log`, tables `v4robust_grid.tsv`, `TFA_gene_scores_with_intervals.csv`.
**A. Robustness.** Stable by the pre-set rule: gate 1e-4 (Spearman 0.87), sum of q (1.00), window -10000..+3000 (0.96). Not stable: gate 1e-6 (0.60), best-hit aggregation (0.62), narrow window -1000..+500 (0.79, AUROC change up to 0.036), 25 reliable motifs (0.62). So the gene ranking depends strongly on how many weak hits are admitted and on the motif set, and only mildly on window size and noisy-OR versus sum. Gate 1e-4 and the reliable motifs scored slightly higher on some tests (e.g. literature 0.623 for gate 1e-4) but the rule fixes the default; no variant is adopted.
**B. Per-gene intervals.** Median 95% interval 74 percentile points wide; 94% of genes are wider than 30 points; only 21% of top-2000 genes stay in the top 2000 in at least 80% of bootstrap draws; also 74 points among genes with at least 3 loci. Individual gene ranks are therefore not reliable; the score is usable for groups of genes (enrichment, gene sets), not for ranking single genes. Released table includes percentile and interval bounds.
**C. Leftover confounds.** rho with promoter CpG obs/exp -0.18 and CpG island fraction -0.14 (flagged; gene density -0.004). TF-A+ (CpG terms added) removes the correlation (-0.05, -0.02) and raises binding ORs in 5 of 6 sets, but lowers response AUROC (largest drop -0.020) and literature AUROC (-0.019), so under the rule it is not adopted; the residual CpG correlation is reported as a limitation.

## Addendum 5 (2026-10-08, written before the run): does prioritising motifs work? Leave-one-dataset-out test
Question: if the motif set is reduced to motifs with reliable binding information, does the training-free score (TF-A, default settings) improve on data that played no part in choosing the motifs?
**Selection (per held-out ChIP set k).** Using only the other 5 of the 6 human ChIP sets and the existing per-motif table `side_reliable_motifs/tables/motif_gc_band_auc_by_set.tsv` (within-GC-band AUC of -log10 FIMO p; AUC per set = mean of the odd and even chromosome values): motif m is reliable_k if its median AUC over the 5 sets is at least 0.55, its AUC exceeds 0.5 in at least 4 of the 5, and it has at least 2,000 hits and a median of at least 50 positive hits in the odd-chromosome table. Set k is not used in any way.
**Scores.** TF-A on all 42 motifs vs TF-A on reliable_k (same gate 1e-5, window, aggregation, residual). Random control: 30 random motif subsets of the size of reliable_k.
**Tests and rule (fixed now).** Prioritising motifs counts as working only if all three hold:
 (a) held-out ChIP set k, gene-level adjusted OR per SD (length, GC, #TSS), all chromosomes: reliable_k is at least all-42 in at least 4 of 6 sets AND exceeds the 90th percentile of its random subsets in at least 3 of 6;
 (b) hepatocyte response (4 contrasts, expressed genes, no overlap with the selection data): paired AUROC difference (the 25-motif set from all 6 ChIP sets minus all 42) has its 95% lower bound above 0 in at least 3 of 4;
 (c) literature genes (tiers 1-3): AUROC of the 25-motif set not more than 0.01 below all 42.
Otherwise motif prioritisation is not adopted and stays a sensitivity analysis.

## Addendum 15 (2026-10-08, written before the run): re-testing motif prioritisation with the benchmarking practice of the field
**Why.** The earlier tests relied on gene-level response in two small hepatocyte datasets and 59 literature genes, with a conjunctive "3 of 4" rule. Published motif and target-gene benchmarks (Kulakovskiy/Vorontsov-style motif benchmarks, e.g. Genome Biology 2020 all-against-all benchmark of 4,972 PWMs; Communications Biology 2025 cross-platform benchmark; target-gene assessments such as Ni et al. 2013 and the TF-perturbation benchmarks) differ in four ways, which are adopted here: (1) motif quality is judged at the **hit level** with auROC/auPRC-type measures against bound versus unbound sequences with **GC-matched negatives**, on data not used to choose motifs; (2) the **gene-level reference is a curated set of verified targets**, with **precision-recall** reported next to ROC and **matched negatives** (not all remaining genes); (3) the **peak-to-gene linking window** is swept, not fixed; (4) conclusions come from effect sizes with intervals across independent datasets, not a count of passed comparisons.
**Data.** Hit level: hits of the 42 motifs (p <= 1e-5) within -10 kb to +3 kb of a TSS (the hits that can enter the gene score), label = hit centre inside a peak of each of the 6 human ChIP sets; GC strata = 10 quantile bands of flank GC. Gene level: PPARgene verified targets (`benchmark/external_data/PPARgene_verified_targets.csv`), **human entries only**: 186 genes (PPARA 58, PPARD 73, PPARG 82; 138 with activation evidence). Primary positives = human genes with activation evidence (138); all 186 and each receptor reported. Not used in any earlier v4 test.
**Motif sets (all fixed earlier, nothing new tuned):** all 42 (reference); reliable-25 (leave-one-dataset-out for the hit-level test, all-set selection for the gene-level test); PPAR-centric (9); no RXR half-sites (20); RXR-half-site-only removal S0 (28); DR1 (22), DR1-Q (20), DR1-NR (5); soft weights (gene-level only).
**Measures.** Hit level: for each set and ChIP set, the Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio of "hit in a peak" for hits of the set versus hits of the removed motifs, stratified by flank-GC band (95% CI), plus raw precision. Gene level: AUROC and average precision (auPRC) for verified targets against all other genes, AUROC against matched negatives (10 negatives per positive matched on promoter length and GC decile, 50 draws), 1,000-resample bootstrap over genes for paired differences versus all 42, adjusted odds ratio per SD (length, GC, log #TSS). Window sweep for all 42 only: -3000..+1000 (default), -10000..+3000, and -1000..+500.
**Rule (fixed now).** A motif set is adopted instead of all 42 only if (a) its Mantel-Haenszel OR versus removed motifs has a lower 95% bound above 1 in at least 4 of the 6 ChIP sets, AND (b) on the primary gold standard its AUROC difference versus all 42 has a lower 95% bound above 0 AND its auPRC difference is positive AND its matched-negative AUROC difference is not negative. Hepatocyte and literature results are not part of this rule (reported earlier). If several sets pass, all are reported and none is preferred automatically. Receptor-specific results and the window sweep are descriptive.

## Addendum 12 (2026-10-08, written before the run): a simpler score
Motivation: the final score has several parts (fixed weight 0.15, linear strength function, exponential distance decay, noisy-OR). Earlier results suggest most are not needed: sum of q gave the same ranking as noisy-OR (Spearman 1.00, Addendum 4), removing distance information changed little (TF-B), and a constant multiplier is absorbed by the intercept of the size-correction regression.
**Candidates (same hits p <= 1e-5, window -3000..+1000, sites merged within 10 bp, same length+GC+TSS residual):**
* **S1 (primary):** R = sum over sites of (s - 4), s = -log10 p clipped 4-9, no distance decay, no 0.15, no noisy-OR; score = standardised residual of ln R.
* **S2 (information only):** R = number of merged sites.
**Rule (fixed now):** S1 counts as equivalent to the final score (TF-A) if (a) Spearman with TF-A is at least 0.95, (b) its AUROC is within 0.01 of TF-A in all 4 hepatocyte contrasts, (c) literature AUROC within 0.02, and (d) the ChIP gene-level adjusted OR ratio S1/TF-A is at least 0.95 in at least 4 of 6 sets. If so the Methods describe S1. Otherwise TF-A stays.

### Addendum 12 result (2026-10-08): the simplest score misses the strict rule by a small margin

Reading: all differences are inside the sampling noise of these data, but the rule was set in advance. What is safe to simplify in the description without any change of result: the 0.15 constant (absorbed by the regression intercept) and the noisy-OR (the sum of weights gave the same ranking, Spearman 1.00, AUROC change at most 0.001; Addendum 4). The recommended wording is therefore: weight each merged site by strength × exponential distance decay, sum over sites, correct for gene size.

## Addendum 13 (2026-10-08, written before the run): an easier-to-explain size correction
Motivation: the hard-to-explain part of the score is the regression with splines that removes the effect of promoter length, GC and number of TSSs. Candidates that a reader can follow in one sentence:
* **A, density:** score = Sum of site weights per kb of promoter (ln of the sum divided by promoter length).
* **B1, size-matched percentile:** each gene is ranked among genes of similar promoter length (deciles of promoter length); the score is the percentile within the decile (0-100).
* **B2, size-and-GC-matched percentile:** each gene is ranked among genes in the same promoter-length decile and promoter-GC tertile (30 groups).
Site weights are those of the final score (strength x exponential decay, summed over sites merged within 10 bp; hits p <= 1e-5; window -3000..+1000), so only the correction changes. Reference: the final score (spline residual).
**Rule (fixed now), per candidate:** it counts as an equivalent, easier replacement if (a) |Spearman| with promoter length, with ln(number of TSSs) and with GC is at most 0.15 for each (the size artefact is removed), (b) AUROC within 0.02 of the final score in all 4 hepatocyte contrasts, (c) literature AUROC within 0.02, and (d) the ChIP gene-level adjusted OR ratio candidate/final is at least 0.90 in at least 4 of 6 sets. If several qualify, the simplest (A, then B1, then B2) is preferred; if none qualifies the spline residual stays and is described as "a regression removes the effect of gene size".
